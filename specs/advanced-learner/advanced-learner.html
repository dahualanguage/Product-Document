Advanced Learner

Self-directed transcription for university & advanced students — with floating subtitle mini-mode

3.7 — ESL Modules Status: Concept Target: TBD Audience: University / Advanced

1 Use Case & Context

Advanced learners — university students or high-grade secondary students — encounter dense academic vocabulary in lectures, seminars, and subject-matter videos. Unlike the K–12 ESL student who works inside a teacher-managed class, this user operates independently: they open a laptop, start recording, and want real-time transcription with word-level support without any instructor setup.

Target User

University student or advanced learner taking content-heavy lectures (science, law, business, etc.) with high academic vocabulary density. No teacher or admin in the loop.

Core Problem

Academic lectures move fast. Students miss or misunderstand specialist terms they haven't encountered before. They need real-time support without disrupting the flow of listening.

2 Session Creation (Student-Initiated)

There is no teacher to create or schedule a session. The student starts directly.

1
Open App & Navigate to "My Sessions"
Student lands on their personal session history. No class roster, no pending invites.
2
Tap "New Session" NEW
Student gives the session a name (e.g. "Bio 301 – Week 4") and selects vocabulary domain. Domain seeds the word-support model with relevant academic terms.
3
Grant Microphone Permission
Browser permission prompt on first use. State is remembered for subsequent sessions.
4
Recording Begins — Full Transcription View
Live transcript appears in real time. Unknown or flagged academic words are highlighted inline. Student can click any word for a popup definition.
Open question: how does the student choose a vocabulary domain?
Options: free text subject tag / preset categories (Science, Law, Business, Medicine…) / auto-detected from transcript. Affects how the AI prioritises which words to flag.

4 Mini-mode / Floating Subtitle Bar

This is the defining feature of 3.7. When the student switches to another browser tab or application (e.g. taking notes in Notion, looking up a concept), the transcription session does not stop — the tab collapses into a persistent floating subtitle bar overlaid on the screen.

Open question: how is mini-mode implemented technically?
Option A — Picture-in-Picture API (native browser, video element required). Option B — always-on-top browser window / popup. Option C — an Electron / desktop app wrapper that can draw over other windows. Each has trade-offs for web portability and permission complexity.
Open question: where does the mini bar appear on screen?
Default position (bottom-center? top-right?), whether it is draggable, and whether its position persists across sessions.
Platform constraint: A standard browser tab cannot render content on top of other tabs or native apps. Mini-mode will require either the Picture-in-Picture API, a separate popup window, or a desktop app wrapper. This needs a technical feasibility decision before design can finalise.

5 Word Interaction & Academic Vocabulary

Academic vocabulary support is deeper than the K–12 word card. University students need more than a translation — they need context.

FieldK–12 Word Card (3.2)Advanced Word Card (3.7)
Definition✓ Chinese translation✓ English definition first
Pronunciation✓✓
Example sentence✓ Simple✓ Academic register
Morphology / word family✗✓ e.g. mitochondria → mitochondrial, mitochondrially
Domain tag✗✓ e.g. [Biology] [Biochemistry]
Save to personal word bank✓ Class word bank✓ Personal word bank (no class)
Open question: does the system auto-flag words, or only respond to clicks?
Auto-flag = AI decides which academic words to highlight proactively (based on domain + difficulty model). Click-only = student gets support only when they explicitly tap a word. Auto-flag is more helpful but risks visual noise in dense transcripts.

6 Session History & Review

After a session ends, the student can review the full transcript and their saved words.

Session list

Student's own sessions sorted by date. Shows session name, duration, word count, and number of saved words.

Session detail

Full transcript with all highlighted words. Student can re-open word cards, add notes, or export the transcript.

Personal word bank

Saved words aggregated across all sessions. Grouped by domain tag. Flashcard-style review (TBD).
