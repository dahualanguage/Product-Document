History

Select a session to view its transcript

In the 20th century, the concept of sovereignty underwent significant transformation in response to the rise of international law.

The Westphalian model, established in 1648, had defined the nation-state as the primary unit of international relations.

However, the aftermath of World War II led to the creation of multilateral institutions such as the United Nations.

These bodies introduced the idea of humanitarian intervention — the notion that state sovereignty is not absolute when governments commit atrocities against their own citizens.

Scholars debate whether such interventions constitute a legitimate limitation on sovereignty or a form of modern imperialism.

The 1990s saw several key cases, including the intervention in Kosovo, that shaped contemporary international norms.
