Personal Word Bank

A student-owned vocabulary collection that aggregates words from live classroom interactions and practice errors, with built-in Mirroring practice for mastery.

ESL Student — 3.2 Status: Planning Updated: Feb 2026

1 Feature Goal

ESL students encounter unfamiliar words across multiple touchpoints — live classes, Mirroring practice, QA Discussion — but have no centralized place to collect, review, and reinforce them.
The Personal Word Bank gives every student a single, persistent vocabulary space that grows from their real learning experiences, with a clear path from "unknown" to "mastered".

The Personal Word Bank is a per-student vocabulary collection that:

  1. Aggregates words from four sources: Live Classroom word clicks, practice errors, Mirroring word tags, and teacher-assigned vocabulary.
  2. Organizes words into two states: To Do (needs practice) and Mastery (learned).
  3. Provides 4 practice types — Spelling, Reading, Content Mirroring, and Multiple Choice — students can practice their word bank anytime.
  4. Tracks mastery using a multi-modal threshold: 5 successful attempts per word, covering at least 3 different practice types.

2 Word Sources

Words enter the Personal Word Bank from four channels:

LIVE Live Classroom

During live class, the subject teacher's speech is transcribed in real-time. Students tap unfamiliar words in the transcript — the system provides context-based definitions. When a student taps, the word is automatically saved to their Personal Word Bank as "To Do".

PRACTICE Practice Errors

When students complete teacher-assigned practices (Mirroring or QA Discussion) and make errors — mispronounced words, unknown vocabulary in AI feedback — those words can be saved to the Word Bank via a "Save to Word Bank" action.

MIRRORING Mirroring Word Tag

During any Mirroring practice (teacher-assigned or Word Bank self-created), students can tag words they don't know directly in the practice text. Tagged words are saved to the Word Bank as "To Do" with the Mirroring sentence as context.

TEACHER Teacher Added

Teachers can manually add words to a student's Word Bank — individually or in bulk to an entire class. This allows teachers to proactively assign target vocabulary (e.g., this week's unit words) that students should practice, even before they encounter them in class.

Connection to Live Classroom (Flow 6): The Live Classroom source directly integrates with the clickable transcript feature. Each word click that shows a definition also saves the word to the Personal Word Bank. The same context sentence is shared for learning reference.

Word Entry Information

Each word saved to the Personal Word Bank includes:

Field Description
Word The English word or phrase
Translation Translation in the student's native language
Context sentence The original sentence where the word appeared
Source Where the word came from: Live Classroom, Practice Error, Mirroring Tag, or Teacher Added
State To Do (needs practice) or Mastery (learned)
Practice progress Number of successful attempts completed (out of 5), and which practice types have been done

3 Word Bank Structure

The Word Bank is divided into two distinct groups:

To Do

Words that need practice. All new words start here. Each word shows its progress (e.g., 3/5 attempts) and which practice types have been completed (icon badges). Words stay in To Do until the student completes 5 successful attempts covering at least 3 different types.

Sorted by: most recently added first

Mastery

Words the student has learned. A word moves here after 5 successful attempts across at least 3 different practice types. Mastery words remain accessible for review but are no longer included in default practice sessions.

Sorted by: most recently mastered first

Mastery Threshold: Why 5 Times Across 3+ Types?

SLA research suggests learners need 6–8 meaningful encounters to acquire a word (Nation 2001, Webb 2007, Horst/Cobb/Meara 1998). We chose 5 attempts rather than 6–8 because each attempt engages a different modality, making each encounter significantly richer than simple repetition:

Practice Type Modality Engaged
Spelling Written recall — actively retrieve the word's form from memory
Reading Spoken production — read the word aloud and receive pronunciation feedback
Mirroring (self-created content) Contextual comprehension + oral production — encounter the word in generated sentences and practice speaking
Multiple Choice Recognition + meaning recall — identify correct meaning/usage among distractors

Requiring at least 3 different types ensures the student has engaged the word through multiple cognitive pathways (written, spoken, contextual, recognition), which research shows produces stronger and more durable vocabulary knowledge than repeating the same task.

Design note: Both the total count (5) and minimum type diversity (3) are configurable at the system level. If future data shows a different optimal combination, it can be adjusted.

4 Practice Mode

Students access practice via a "Practice" button in the top-right corner of the Word Bank page. The system offers 4 practice types, each engaging a different learning modality.

Builds on existing Vocabulary Practice: The platform already has a Vocabulary Practice system with card-based UI, animated card stack, recording controls, star ratings, and graded pronunciation feedback (GradedSentence). Word Bank practice reuses these UI patterns and components.

Word Selection (Before Practice Starts)

After choosing a practice type, students see a word selection screen with the header: "Uncheck the words you'd like to skip."

  1. All To Do words are listed with checkboxes (all checked by default)
  2. Each word shows: word/phrase and translation
  3. Student can uncheck words they want to skip this session
  4. Pagination buttons: "上5題" / "下5題" for navigating long word lists
  5. Tap "Start" to begin with the selected words

This gives students control over their practice — for example, focusing on a specific set of words before a test, or skipping words they recently practiced.

4a. Spelling

Modality: Written recall

  1. Card shows: context sentence (target word replaced by _____) + translation/definition
  2. Student types the correct English word
  3. If correct → success sound effect + green animation → counts as 1 attempt
  4. If incorrect → error sound effect + correct spelling shown briefly (red highlight) → does not count

4b. Reading

Modality: Spoken production

  1. Card shows the word + plays TTS audio (Azure, student's preferred speed)
  2. Student taps Record and reads the word aloud
  3. System shows color-coded pronunciation feedback (GradedSentence: green/orange/red) + star rating
  4. Student can Redo or Continue → completing a recording counts as 1 attempt

4c. Content Mirroring (Self-Created Content)

Modality: Contextual comprehension + oral production

  1. Student taps "Start Practice" and selects Content Mirroring
  2. Word selection screen — student unchecks words to skip, then confirms
  3. "Creating Content" loading screen — system generates a short passage that naturally incorporates the selected vocabulary words
  4. "Your article is ready — personalized to your level and word bank" preview screen appears:
    • Shows the generated article with target words highlighted
    • Each sentence includes an L1 translation alongside the English text
    • Student can Accept or Reject the article
    • If Reject → system generates a new article with the same words
    • If Accept → tap "Let's start" to begin Mirroring practice
  5. Student does Mirroring practice with the accepted article — listen to each sentence, then repeat aloud
  6. System evaluates pronunciation with color-coded feedback + star rating
  7. Each vocabulary word that appears in the generated content and is successfully mirrored counts as 1 Mirroring attempt for that word
Key benefit: Students encounter their vocabulary words in natural context, not in isolation. The generated sentences reinforce both the word's meaning and its usage patterns. This is the richest practice type because it combines reading comprehension, listening, and speaking.

4d. Multiple Choice

Modality: Recognition + meaning recall

  1. System generates MC questions from the student's To Do words (AI-generated distractors)
  2. Question types may include: English → Translation, Translation → English, Context sentence → correct word, Audio → correct word
  3. Student selects an answer
  4. If correct → success sound effect → counts as 1 attempt
  5. If incorrect → error sound effect + correct answer highlighted → does not count

Practice Rules

Rule Detail
Mastery threshold 5 successful attempts per word, covering at least 3 different practice types
What counts as "successful" Spelling correct, pronunciation recorded, Mirroring completed, or MC answered correctly. No minimum pronunciation score required.
Incorrect attempt Correct answer is revealed. Does not count as a successful attempt.
Type diversity Student must use at least 3 of 4 practice types for a word to reach Mastery. The remaining 2 attempts can be any type.
Progress display Each word card shows: attempt count (X/5) and icon badges for completed practice types (✎ 🔊 🎤 ✓). Students can immediately see what types they still need.
Practice type selection Student chooses which type from the "Start Practice ▼" dropdown. System highlights types they haven't tried yet for incomplete words.
Mastery transition Immediate — word moves to Mastery as soon as 5th attempt completes (with 3+ types satisfied)
Re-practice mastered words Students can manually move a Mastery word back to To Do if they want to review

5 Attempt Tracking

Every Personal Word Bank practice session is recorded as an attempt record, visible to both the student and their teachers.

What gets recorded per session

  • Which words the student practiced and which practice type was used
  • Result per word (correct / incorrect)
  • Pronunciation scores (for Reading and Mirroring types)
  • How many successful attempts each word had before and after the session
  • How many distinct practice types each word has completed (towards the 3-type requirement)
  • Whether any word reached mastery (5 attempts + 3 types) in this session
  • Session summary: total words practiced, accuracy rate, new mastery count
Teacher visibility: Attempt records appear in the teacher's dashboard alongside regular practice records. Teachers can see which students are actively reviewing their Word Bank and how their vocabulary is growing. Word Bank practice sessions are distinguished from teacher-assigned practice attempts.

Relationship with existing attempt system

The platform already supports versioned attempt tracking for teacher-assigned practices (Mirroring and QA Discussion). Each practice attempt creates a separate history record with scores, conversations, and AI summaries.

Personal Word Bank practice sessions should integrate with the same attempt tracking system where possible, so that:

  • Teachers see a unified view of all student practice activity (assigned + self-initiated)
  • Self-initiated Word Bank sessions are clearly labeled as such
  • The existing Data Dashboard can display Word Bank progress without building a separate reporting pipeline

6 Integration Points

6a. Live Classroom → Word Bank

  1. Subject teacher speaks → speech is transcribed in real-time
  2. Student taps a word in the live transcript
  3. Popup shows translation/definition based on context
  4. Simultaneously: the word is auto-saved to Personal Word Bank as "To Do" (source: Live Classroom)
  5. No duplicate — if the word already exists in the bank, the context sentence is updated with the newer occurrence

6b. Practice Errors → Word Bank

  1. Student completes a Mirroring or QA practice assigned by teacher
  2. For Mirroring: words with low pronunciation scores are highlighted
  3. For QA Discussion: when AI provides a correction sentence (Mirroring mode in QA), the key vocabulary is identified
  4. Student sees a "Save to Word Bank" button next to flagged words
  5. Tapping saves the word to the Word Bank (source: Practice Error) with the practice context sentence

6c. Mirroring Practice → Word Bank (Word Tagging)

  1. Student is doing any Mirroring practice (teacher-assigned or Word Bank self-created content)
  2. The practice text displays on screen — student can tap/click any word they don't know
  3. Tapped word is highlighted and a "Save to Word Bank" tooltip appears
  4. Confirming saves the word to the bank (source: Mirroring Tag) with the Mirroring sentence as context
  5. Student continues the Mirroring practice without interruption
Why this matters: Mirroring practice surfaces words students struggle with in real spoken context. Letting them tag unknown words during practice captures the learning moment when the word is most salient.

6d. Word Bank → Data Dashboard

  • Total words in To Do / Mastery per student
  • Mastery rate over time (words mastered per week)
  • Practice frequency (Word Bank sessions per week)
  • Word sources breakdown (% from Live Classroom vs Practice Errors)

8d. Teacher Assign Flow (Add Words in Bulk)

Multi-step flow for teachers to assign vocabulary to students' Word Banks. Can be started from the dashboard or from an individual student's view.

Step 1 — Choose Word Source

Teacher selects where to pull words from:

Source Description
From Projects Browse existing project content and select vocabulary words from articles/passages
From Practice List Pull words from an existing Mirroring or QA practice's content
From Assignments Extract vocabulary from a previously created assignment
Add Manually Type words/phrases directly into a text input field, one at a time

Step 2 — Select / Enter Words

  • From content source: system displays extracted words with checkboxes. Teacher selects the words to include.
  • Manual entry: Teacher types a Word / Phrase into a text field. System auto-generates translation. Teacher can click "Add" to append each word to the list. Repeat for multiple words.
  • Both modes can be combined — pull some words from a project and add others manually.

Step 3 — Review and Confirm

A review table shows all selected words with auto-detected Part of Speech tags (Noun, Verb, Adjective, etc.). Teacher can:

  • Edit part of speech if auto-detection is wrong
  • Remove words from the list before confirming
  • See total word count that will be assigned

Step 4 — Select Classes and Students

"Active Classes and Students" screen:

  • Teacher selects target class(es) from a dropdown
  • Student list appears with checkboxes — select all or pick individual students
  • Shows count: "N words will be added to each selected student's Word Bank"
  • Click "Assign Students" to execute

Step 5 — Confirmation

Success screen: "These words are now in the student's Word Bank."

  • "Back to student's word bank" — return to dashboard
  • "Assign more words" — restart the flow

9 Rules Summary

Game Rules — displayed in-app to students

  1. Words you tap in Live Classroom are automatically saved to your Word Bank.
  2. Words you get wrong in practice can be manually saved to your Word Bank.
  3. Your teacher can also add words for you to practice.
  4. All new words start in "To Do".
  5. There are 4 ways to practice: ✎ Spelling, 🔊 Reading, 🎤 Content Mirroring, ✓ Multiple Choice.
  6. Each correct practice = 1 successful attempt. Wrong answers don't count — but you'll see the correct answer!
  7. To master a word, you need 5 successful attempts using at least 3 different practice types.
  8. Check the badges on each word (✎ Spelling, 🔊 Reading, 🎤 Content Mirroring, ✓ MC) to see which types you've done and what you still need.
  9. After mastering a word, it moves to "Mastery".
  10. Your practice records are saved — your teacher can see your progress!
Open question: Should we implement spaced repetition (requiring attempts to be spread across multiple days) or keep it simple (5 attempts regardless of timing)? The current spec uses the simpler model for MVP. Spaced repetition could be added as a v2 enhancement.
