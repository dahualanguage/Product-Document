Personal Word Bank

A student-owned vocabulary collection that aggregates words from live classroom interactions and practice errors, with built-in Mirroring practice for mastery.

ESL Student — 3.2 Status: Planning Updated: Mar 2026 (v9)

1 Feature Goal

ESL students encounter unfamiliar words across multiple touchpoints — live classes, post-class practice, teacher assignments — but have no centralized place to collect, review, and reinforce them.
The Personal Word Bank gives every student a single, persistent vocabulary space that grows from their real learning experiences, with a clear path from "unknown" to "mastered".

The Personal Word Bank is a per-student vocabulary collection that:

  1. Aggregates words from three sources: Live Classroom word clicks, Practice (Vocabs Mirroring / Vocabs Multiple Choice / any Mirroring word tagging), and Teacher-assigned vocabulary.
  2. Organizes words into two states: Learning (needs practice) and Mastered (learned).
  3. Provides 3 core practice types — Spelling, Vocabs Mirroring, and Multiple Choice — plus a Practice All mode that randomly assigns a type per word. Students can practice their word bank anytime.
  4. Tracks mastery using a multi-modal threshold: 5 successful attempts per word, covering all 3 core practice types.

3 Word Bank Structure

The Word Bank is divided into two distinct groups:

Learning

Words that need practice. All new words start here. Each word shows its progress (e.g., 3/5 attempts) and which practice types have been completed (icon badges). Words stay in Learning until the student completes 5 successful attempts covering at least 3 different types.

Sorted by: most recently added first

Mastered

Words the student has learned. A word moves here after 5 successful attempts covering all 3 practice types. Mastered words remain accessible for review but are no longer included in default practice sessions.

Sorted by: most recently mastered first

Translation Toggle

The word card has a toggle switch (top-right) that controls translation visibility:

  • Off (default): only English word, definition, and example are shown
  • On: Chinese translation appears inline after the English word; definition translation and example translation also display below their English counterparts; a play button for native-language pronunciation appears
Design rationale: Defaulting to English-only encourages target-language processing. Students toggle translation on demand when they need L1 support, rather than relying on it.

Homepage UI Elements

Mastery Explanation Popup (“How it works”)

Triggered by the “How it works” button in the notice banner. A floating panel that explains the mastery system to students:

  • “The Power of 5” — Each word needs 5 correct practices to move from Learning to Mastered
  • “Mix Your Practice” — Must practice in every mode at least once (Spelling, Multiple Choice, Mirroring)
  • “Got it” CTA button closes the panel

Confirm Reset Popup

Triggered by the Retry button on a mastered word’s card. Asks the student to confirm before resetting:

  • Title: “Move Back to Learning?”
  • Description: “This will reset all progress for [WORD]. You’ll need to complete 5 practices again to master it.”
  • Actions: Cancel (keep state) / “Yes, Reset” (revert to Learning, zero all progress)
v9 change: The previous “Back to Learning” button has been replaced by a Retry/Reset mechanism with an explicit confirmation step to prevent accidental resets.

Mastery Threshold: Why 5 Times Across 3+ Types?

SLA research suggests learners need 6–8 meaningful encounters to acquire a word (Nation 2001, Webb 2007, Horst/Cobb/Meara 1998). We chose 5 attempts rather than 6–8 because each attempt engages a different modality, making each encounter significantly richer than simple repetition:

Practice Types

Practice TypeModality Engaged
a. Spelling Written recall — actively retrieve the word's form from memory
b. Vocabs Mirroring Spoken production — say the word and receive pronunciation feedback (reuses existing Vocabs Mirroring module)
c. Multiple Choice Recognition + meaning recall — identify correct meaning/usage among distractors

Requiring all 3 practice types ensures the student has engaged the word through multiple cognitive pathways (written, spoken, recognition), which research shows produces stronger and more durable vocabulary knowledge than repeating the same task.

Design note: Both the total count (5) and minimum type diversity (3) are configurable at the system level. If future data shows a different optimal combination, it can be adjusted.

4 Practice Mode

The system offers 3 core practice types (Spelling, Vocabs Mirroring, Multiple Choice), each engaging a different learning modality, plus a Practice All mode that randomly assigns one of the 3 types per word.

Builds on existing Vocabulary Practice: The platform already has a Vocabulary Practice system with card-based UI, animated card stack, recording controls, star ratings, and graded pronunciation feedback (GradedSentence). Word Bank practice reuses these UI patterns and components.

Written recall

Duration → Word count: 3 min → 6 words  |  5 min → 10  |  10 min → 15  |  15 min → 20

  1. System presents a context sentence with the target word blanked out
  2. Student types the correct English word
  3. Timer: 60 seconds per question; warning at 10s remaining; timeout = wrong
  4. 3-strike progressive hints:
    • 1st wrong → show translation + definition
    • 2nd wrong → show first letter hint
    • 3rd wrong → reveal answer and move on
  5. Scoring: only 1st-attempt correct = Correct; any hint used = Wrong

Spoken production — 2-step per word (word + sentence)

Duration → Word count: 3 min → 6 words  |  5 min → 10  |  10 min → 15  |  15 min → 20

  1. Each word has 2 steps: first say the word, then say the example sentence
  2. Card shows the target text (word or sentence) with Chinese translation; no phonetic/pinyin
  3. Student taps the mic button to record, taps again to submit
  4. Timer: 10 seconds for word step, 20 seconds for sentence step; warning at 5s remaining; timeout = wrong
  5. System scores pronunciation with 5-star rating (≥3 stars = pass, <3 = fail)
  6. Hint: speaker/play button is hidden initially; appears after 1st failed attempt as a pronunciation hint
  7. 3-strike rule: 3 failed recordings → auto-advance to next step/word
  8. Scoring: 1st-attempt pass = Correct; pass after hint(s) = Wrong; 3 fails = Wrong

Recognition + meaning recall

Duration → Word count: 3 min → 10 words  |  5 min → 15  |  10 min → 20  |  15 min → 35

  1. System presents a context sentence with the target word blanked out, plus 4 options (1 correct + 3 AI-generated distractors)
  2. Timer: 20 seconds per question; warning at 5s remaining; timeout = wrong (reveals answer)
  3. Progressive hints on wrong answers:
    • 1st attempt → no hints
    • 2nd attempt (after 1st wrong) → show definition
    • 3rd attempt (after 2nd wrong) → show definition + translation
  4. 3rd wrong: correct answer is revealed and auto-advance
  5. Scoring: only 1st-attempt correct = Correct; any wrong attempt used = Wrong

Random assignment — each word gets one of the 3 core types

Duration → Word count: same as Spelling (3 min → 6  |  5 min → 10  |  10 min → 15  |  15 min → 20)

  1. Type assignment: system randomly assigns each word a practice type (Spelling, Vocabs Mirroring, or Multiple Choice) with equal distribution across the 3 types
  2. Each word follows the exact same rules as its assigned type (timer, hints, scoring, strikes)
  3. Mastery counting: each word's attempt counts toward the assigned type — e.g., a word assigned Spelling in Practice All counts as a Spelling attempt
  4. Quick Vocab Preview: shows all words before practice starts; practice type indicators are hidden (student discovers each word's type during practice)
  5. Scoring: follows the assigned type's scoring rules; result page shows the practice type badge per word
Why Practice All? It helps students who are close to Mastery by automatically filling in missing practice types. Instead of manually choosing 3 separate sessions, one Mixed session can cover all types.

5 Attempt Tracking

Every Personal Word Bank practice session is recorded as an attempt record, visible to both the student and their teachers.

What gets recorded per session

  • Which words the student practiced and which practice type was used
  • Result per word (correct / incorrect)
  • Pronunciation scores (for Vocabs Mirroring)
  • How many successful attempts each word had before and after the session
  • How many distinct practice types each word has completed (towards the 3-type requirement)
  • Whether any word reached mastery (5 attempts + 3 types) in this session
  • Session summary: total words practiced, accuracy rate, new mastery count
Teacher visibility: Attempt records appear in the teacher's dashboard alongside regular practice records. Teachers can see which students are actively reviewing their Word Bank and how their vocabulary is growing. Word Bank practice sessions are distinguished from teacher-assigned practice attempts.

Relationship with existing attempt system

The platform already supports versioned attempt tracking for teacher-assigned practices (Mirroring and QA Discussion). Each practice attempt creates a separate history record with scores, conversations, and AI summaries.

Personal Word Bank practice sessions should integrate with the same attempt tracking system where possible, so that:

  • Teachers see a unified view of all student practice activity (assigned + self-initiated)
  • Self-initiated Word Bank sessions are clearly labeled as such
  • The existing Data Dashboard can display Word Bank progress without building a separate reporting pipeline

6 Integration Points

6a. Live Classroom → Word Bank

  1. Subject teacher speaks → speech is transcribed in real-time
  2. Student taps a word in the live transcript
  3. Popup shows translation/definition based on context
  4. Simultaneously: the word is auto-saved to Personal Word Bank as “Learning” (source: Live Classroom)
  5. No duplicate — if the word already exists in the bank, the context sentence is updated with the newer occurrence

6b. Practice Errors → Word Bank

  1. Student completes a Mirroring practice assigned by teacher
  2. Words with low pronunciation scores are highlighted
  3. Student sees a "Save to Word Bank" button next to flagged words
  4. Tapping saves the word to the Word Bank (source: Practice) with the practice context sentence

6c. Mirroring Practice → Word Bank (Word Tagging)

  1. Student is doing any Mirroring practice (teacher-assigned or Word Bank self-created content)
  2. The practice text displays on screen — student can tap/click any word they don't know
  3. Tapped word is highlighted and a "Save to Word Bank" tooltip appears
  4. Confirming saves the word to the bank (source: Practice) with the Mirroring sentence as context
  5. Student continues the Mirroring practice without interruption
Why this matters: Mirroring practice surfaces words students struggle with in real spoken context. Letting them tag unknown words during practice captures the learning moment when the word is most salient.

6d. Word Bank → Data Dashboard

  • Total words in Learning / Mastered per student
  • Mastery rate over time (words mastered per week)
  • Practice frequency (Word Bank sessions per week)
  • Word sources breakdown (% from Live Classroom vs Practice vs Teacher Added)

8 Rules Summary

Core Rules

  1. Words tapped in Live Classroom are automatically saved to the Word Bank.
  2. Words answered incorrectly in practice can be manually saved to the Word Bank.
  3. Teachers can also add words to a student's Word Bank.
  4. All new words start in “Learning”.
  5. There are 3 practice types: Spelling, Vocabs Mirroring, and Multiple Choice.
  6. Each correct practice = 1 successful attempt. Incorrect answers do not count.
  7. To master a word: 5 successful attempts using all 3 practice types.
  8. After mastering a word, it moves to “Mastered”.
  9. All practice records are saved and visible to teachers.
Decision (v9): Spaced repetition is not included in the current design. The system uses the simpler model (5 attempts regardless of timing). The previous “Keep it Fresh” reminder concept was removed from the mastery popup. Spaced repetition remains a candidate for a future enhancement.

9 Future: Contextual Mirroring (Next Phase)

Not in current scope. Contextual Mirroring is planned as the 4th practice type in a future release. It was removed from the homepage practice buttons in v9 (previously shown as the 4th button in the bento grid). The sections below document the intended design for reference.

Overview

Contextual comprehension + oral production — the richest practice type, combining reading comprehension, listening, and speaking in natural context.

Flow

  1. Student selects a word to practice
  2. System auto-generates multiple example sentences demonstrating the word in different real-world contexts
  3. Student browses the example sentences and practices speaking each one aloud
  4. System evaluates pronunciation with graded feedback
  5. Each sentence successfully spoken counts as 1 Contextual Mirroring attempt for that word
Key benefit: Students encounter their vocabulary words in natural context, not in isolation. The generated sentences reinforce both the word's meaning and its usage patterns.

Impact on Mastery Rules When Added

When Contextual Mirroring is introduced as the 4th practice type, the mastery threshold should be re-evaluated:

  • Current: 5 attempts covering all 3 types
  • Proposed: 5 attempts covering at least 3 of 4 types (restores flexibility)
  • Contextual Mirroring skips the “Question Count” step (not applicable)

Design Notes

  • Contextual Mirroring reuses the existing Mirroring practice UI (card stack, recording controls, GradedSentence feedback)
  • Example sentences are AI-generated per word, refreshed each session for variety
  • Pronunciation scores from Contextual Mirroring sessions are included in attempt tracking

Word Deletion (Student)

  • Students can delete words from their Word Bank
  • Deletion rules TBD: which states allow deletion (Learning only? Mastered too?), soft-delete vs permanent, recovery option
  • Teacher dashboard would show “Removed Words” count per student to flag potential disengagement

Teacher Override (Move Between Groups)

  • Teachers can manually move a word from Learning to Mastered (or vice versa)
  • Rules TBD: how override interacts with attempt progress (reset count? preserve history?), audit trail for overrides
  • Also includes teacher ability to remove words from a student’s bank
