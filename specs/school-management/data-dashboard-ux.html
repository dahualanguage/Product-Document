1 Navigation Flow

How does a user navigate between the three main views of the dashboard?
The dashboard has 3 screens: Overview (landing), Group/Cohort View, and Individual Student View. Users must be able to move between them without dead ends.

Screen Transition Map

FromActionTo
Dashboard OverviewClick any Summary CardGroup View (pre-filtered by card type)
Dashboard OverviewApply filters in Filter BarGroup View (filtered)
Dashboard OverviewSearch student nameIndividual Student View
Dashboard OverviewClick student in Risk ListIndividual Student View
Dashboard OverviewClick saved cohort chipGroup View (cohort loaded)
Group ViewClick student rowIndividual Student View
Group ViewChange filtersGroup View (re-filtered)
Group View“Save as Cohort”Cohort creation dialog → Group View
Group ViewBack buttonDashboard Overview
Individual ViewSwitch tabSame screen, different tab
Individual ViewBack buttonPrevious screen (Overview or Group)
No dead ends: Every screen has a back path. The Individual View preserves the previous context — if the user came from Group View, “Back” returns to Group View with filters intact. If they came from Overview, “Back” returns to Overview.

7 Edge States & Empty States

What does the designer need to account for when there’s no data, partial data, or error states?
These states are often overlooked but are critical for a polished product. Every screen must handle them.
StateConditionUI Treatment
First-time empty School just onboarded, no students enrolled yet All Summary Cards show 0 / empty. Show illustration + message: “Enroll students and assign practice to start seeing data.” Link to Classroom Management.
Insufficient data Student has < 3 completed learning sessions Grey placeholder on all metric values. Trend lines hidden. Message: “Need at least 3 sessions to show trends.” Show progress bar: “2/3 sessions completed.”
No filter results Applied filters match 0 students Empty state in Group View: “No students match these filters.” Show active filter pills + “Clear all” button.
Tier change alert Student moves from Tier 2 → Tier 3 (confirmed after 2 evals) Red badge dot on Risk Alerts card. Student appears in Risk List with “NEW” tag. Optional: browser notification if enabled.
Pending tier change Student met Tier 3 criteria once, waiting for 2nd eval Show “Pending” tag next to tier in Individual View. Tooltip: “Will be confirmed if criteria persist in next evaluation.”
Loading state Data is being fetched Skeleton placeholders (shimmer animation) on all cards and charts. Never show blank/empty during load.
Error state API call fails Inline error message per section (not full-page). “Unable to load data. Try again.” + retry button. Other sections still functional.

8 Dashboard Presets & Customization (MVP)

How can teachers personalize their dashboard without needing to re-filter every time?
Different ESL teachers focus on different metrics. Instead of forcing everyone through the same default layout, we provide preset templates + widget-level control. This is the MVP approach — full drag-and-drop layout editing and scheduled digest emails are deferred to a future phase.

8.1 Preset Templates

Teachers choose one preset as their default dashboard. The system remembers their choice and loads it on every visit.

PresetSummary Cards ShownPrimary FocusBest For
Overview Default All 4 cards + Risk List Balanced snapshot of all metrics School admins, teachers new to the dashboard
At-Risk Focus Risk Alerts + Tier Distribution + expanded Risk List Tier 3 & declining students Teachers monitoring struggling students
Progress Tracker Completion Overview + Group Progress chart + Tier Distribution Completion rates & trend direction Teachers tracking assignment follow-through
Language Focus Language Access Rate + Common Barriers table + Difficulty breakdown Vocabulary & comprehension gaps Teachers focused on language scaffolding

8.2 Widget Pinning

After selecting a preset, teachers can further customize by pinning or unpinning individual widgets. Changes auto-save to their user profile.

9 Saved Cohorts (Cross-Classroom Groups)

How does an ESL teacher track a group of students who share the same proficiency level but are spread across different classrooms?
ESL teachers don’t think in homeroom classes — they think in proficiency cohorts. A teacher might monitor “all Grade 5 WIDA Level 4 students in Science” even though those 12 students sit in 3 different classrooms. Saved Cohorts let teachers define, name, and persist these cross-classroom groups.

9.1 What is a Saved Cohort?

A Saved Cohort is a named, persistent student group that a teacher creates by combining filter criteria with optional manual student adjustments. Unlike ephemeral filters, cohorts are saved to the teacher’s profile and can be loaded with a single click on any subsequent visit.

Cohort vs. Filter vs. Classroom

ConceptScopePersistenceCross-classroom?
ClassroomFixed student rosterPermanent (until enrollment changes)No — single class only
FilterDynamic querySession only (localStorage)Yes
Saved CohortNamed group = filters + manual overridesServer-side, persistentYes

9.6 Use Case Examples

Example 1 — Cross-classroom Science cohort

Ms. Chen teaches ESL push-in for 5th grade Science across rooms 3A, 3B, and 3C. She creates a cohort “G5 W4 — Sci” with filters Grade 5 + WIDA 4 + Science. This captures 12 students across all 3 classrooms. She also manually adds 2 WIDA 3 students who are borderline. Every Monday, she clicks this cohort to check if they share common vocabulary barriers (e.g., “photosynthesis” flagged for 10/14 students).

Example 2 — Dual-subject tracking

Mr. Park monitors the same group of newcomer students across both Science and Social Studies. He creates two cohorts: “Newcomers — Sci” and “Newcomers — SS” with the same students but different subject filters. He compares whether students who struggle in Science vocabulary also struggle in Social Studies vocabulary, informing his cross-curricular scaffolding strategy.

10 Responsive Considerations

BreakpointLayout Changes
> 1024px (desktop)4 Summary Cards in row. Side-by-side gauge + trend in Individual View. Full filter bar visible.
768px – 1024px (tablet)2 Summary Cards per row. Individual View tabs become scrollable horizontal strip. Filter bar collapses to “Filters” button with dropdown panel.
< 768px (mobile)1 Summary Card per row (stacked). Tabs become a dropdown selector. Charts reduce to simplified sparklines. Risk list becomes card-based layout.
Chart library note: Whichever chart library is used (Recharts, Chart.js, etc.), all charts must support touch interactions (swipe to pan, pinch to zoom on trend lines) and show tooltips on tap for mobile.
