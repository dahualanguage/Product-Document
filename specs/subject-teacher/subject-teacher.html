Subject Teacher

Content class ESL support — live transcription, student roster, word tap insights, and session history.

ESL Module — 3.4 Status: Planning Updated: April 2026

1 Feature Goal

How does a content-class teacher who teaches science/math in English support ESL students in real-time, without disrupting the teaching flow?
Goal: During a live content class, capture the teacher’s speech in real time, convert it to text, and deliver it to students as an interactive transcript. After class, provide the teacher with insights into which vocabulary students found difficult.

The Subject Teacher module provides four core capabilities:

  • Live Class — Start a live transcription session, manage mic test, pause/resume, and end class with automatic save.
  • Student Roster — View enrolled students with grade, English level, native language, and attendance data.
  • Word Tap Insights — Review which words students tapped during live sessions, with per-student breakdowns and cross-session trends.
  • Session History — Browse past sessions, view metadata, word tap summaries, and full transcripts.

3 Live Class

3.1 Pre-class Mic Test

Before starting a live session the teacher must complete a microphone voice test. The flow diagram below illustrates the full Subject Teacher session lifecycle.

View Flow Diagram — Subject Teacher
100%

      

    

  


  
The following scenarios describe each state the microphone test can reach.


  

    
ScenarioScreenTextBehaviour

    
      

        1. Microphone Unconnected (no device)
        No microphone hardware detected
        “Microphone unconnected — Please allow Microphone access in your browser, or check that your device is connected.”
        Voice test cannot proceed. Block session start.
      

      

        2. Permission Denied (Browser settings)
        Browser mic permission dialog was dismissed or rejected
        “Microphone permission is required. Please enable it in your browser settings.”
        The system cannot detect any audio input. The teacher must enable microphone permission in their browser settings before proceeding.
      

      

        3. Connected — Ready to Test
        Microphone Connected
        “Speak to test” — “Test 1, 2, 3”
        Display test phrase prompt. Await teacher voice input.
      

      

        4. Connected — Voice Test Active
        Teacher is speaking; transcribed words enlarge in the transcription box
        Each correctly transcribed word becomes enlarged in the transcription box.
        Confirms: (a) audio is being recorded, (b) speech-to-text is functioning, (c) the transcription pipeline is working end-to-end.
      

      

        5. Voice Test Passed — Ready to Start
        Test phrase successfully transcribed
        Test phrase remains visible as confirmation.
        The “Start Class” button appears. Teacher can begin the live session.
      

      

        6. Voice Test Failed — Low Volume
        Microphone input volume too low to transcribe accurately
        “Volume too low — please move closer to the microphone or increase input volume.”
        Prompt the teacher to adjust microphone position or volume level before retrying.
      

      

        7. Voice Test Failed — Mismatch
        Transcribed output is significantly different from the expected test phrase
        “Voice test did not match. Please try again in a quieter environment.”
        The microphone is recording and text is appearing, but the words do not match the expected phrase. Prompt the teacher to retry or check mic input source.
      

      

        8. STT Service Unavailable
        Microphone is working but Speech-to-Text service is down or unreachable
        “Transcription service is currently unavailable. Please try again later.”
        Falls back to Scenario 1 behaviour — voice test cannot proceed, block session start. Retry button available.
      

    
  


  

    Shared spec: These scenarios are shared with the Live Classroom spec. The mic test is identical for all teachers.

Should the Subject Teacher see a live count of student word taps during class, or only after class ends?

    
Showing a live tap count could help the teacher gauge comprehension in real time, but it may also be distracting during active teaching.

Should the Subject Teacher see the student’s full word bank, or only words tapped in their own sessions?

    
Full visibility helps the teacher understand the student’s overall vocabulary level. Scoped visibility protects student privacy and keeps the view focused.

5.2 Session Detail


  
Clicking a session card shows detailed word tap analysis:

  
    
    
  • Word list sorted by tap frequency — most-tapped words at the top
    • 
    
  • Per-student breakdown — which students tapped which words
    • 
    
  • Transcript excerpt — showing where each word appeared in context
    • 
  


  

    
Should the per-student breakdown show student names, or anonymized identifiers (Student A, B, C)?

    
Named breakdowns help the teacher provide targeted support. Anonymized breakdowns protect student privacy and reduce potential bias.

  


  
  
5.3 Aggregated View


  
Cross-session trends provide a broader picture of vocabulary difficulty patterns:

  
    
    
  • Most-tapped words across all sessions — cumulative ranking showing persistent vocabulary challenges
    • 
    
  • Students who tap most frequently — may indicate lower English proficiency and a need for additional support
    • 
    
  • Vocabulary trends over time — new difficult words vs. recurring ones, helping identify whether students are progressing
    • 
  


  

    
Is the aggregated view a Subject Teacher feature, or ESL Teacher-only?

    
Subject Teachers may benefit from seeing trends in their own sessions. However, cross-teacher aggregation is likely ESL Teacher-only to avoid data leakage between classrooms.

6.3 Transcript View


  
A read-only scrollable transcript with timestamp markers. Words that students tapped are highlighted with a distinct background color and a tap count badge, making it easy to see which parts of the lesson caused difficulty.


  

    
Should the transcript support audio playback sync, or text-only for MVP?

    
Audio playback would let teachers hear the exact moment a word was spoken, which is useful for reviewing pronunciation and context. Text-only is simpler to implement and still provides value.
